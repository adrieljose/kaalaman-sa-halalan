  What changed

  All 11 characters are now in true 3/4 battle poses, not the flat profile from

  exactly what the flat profile was costing.

  composition now reads JUAN → ← ENEMY instead of everyone posing for the
  camera.

  rivals lean left, and hits recoil away from whoever landed them. All 14 rivals
  across both chapters resolve, and no per-skill override can snap a rival back
  to front-facing mid-attack.

  Two things worth knowing

  A sizing trap I caught before it shipped. The character TextureRects use
  KEEP_ASPECT_CENTERED, so a shorter canvas gets scaled up to fill the node. The
  new poses cropped to anywhere from 132 to 171px tall, which would have made
  some rivals render noticeably larger than others purely by accident of
  cropping. Everything is padded to a common 180-tall canvas instead of scaled —
  so the Ogre still towers over Fredo for the right reason.

  Maria's flip_h had to go. It existed only because her old front-on frames
  faced the wrong way. Mirroring frames that already face the rival would have
  turned her back on him again.

  One thing I did not do

  Chapter 1's five rivals still face the camera. Since the player change is
  global, Chapter 1 now reads as Juan confronting a Lord Trapo who's posing —
  visible in the bottom row of the second image. It's arguably better than both
  facing front, but it is inconsistent, and Chapter 1 is the chapter that's 
  currently live.

  Fixing it is the same recipe and about 10 more generations; I have 8, so I'd
  rather not start it and leave you with three of five done. Say the word when
  there's headroom and I'll finish that set.

  1. What changed

  A BattleShadows layer draws a soft contact shadow under every fighter. It's a
  sibling of the characters, never a child — a child would inherit the body's
  transform, and the combat choreography leans, rotates and scales that
  transform constantly, so the shadow would tilt off the floor and lean with the
  punch. Everything is derived from each subject's live rect every frame, so
  nothing has to be told when a character moves, when a skill throws it across
  the stage, when the layout changes, or when the next rival is swapped in.

  Its tree index is the whole of its correctness: immediately below the player
  is the only slot where a shadow lands on the backdrop and its props while
